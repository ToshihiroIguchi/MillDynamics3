## Survey findings to base the "prior work / novelty" text on
(wording must stay narrow: "to our knowledge", "in the sources we found")
- Mill DEM (Mishra & Rajamani; Cleary) is offline and dry; wet mill work (Cleary 2006/2020 DEM+SPH, Sinnott/Jayasundara stirred mills, Doroszuk 2024 DualSPHysics) is offline, 3D, and slurry coupling is usually one-way or porous-media (two-way for Cleary 2006 not verified). Commercial tools (Rocky DEM: CFD-DEM/SPH per a vendor snippet; EDEM and Millsoft capabilities UNVERIFIED) are desktop/offline.
- Lubrication in DEM for wet milling exists (Cabiscol 2021, de Klerk 2026), as DEM-only force models; here the same idea (Reynolds squeeze film, classical Brenner/Cox-Brenner, lattice-Boltzmann corrections Nguyen & Ladd, Kempe & Frohlich, Costa) is combined with a particle fluid solver and calibrated on a resolved grid solver.
- Coarse-graining: Sakai 2009 (conveying), Bierwisch 2009 (die filling), Yamamoto 2012 (wet bead milling, enlarged particle). No mass-preserving rule tied to fluid resolution and sub-step count in a tumbling mill found.
- No browser/WASM mill or DEM simulator, and no interactive/real-time grinding-mill process simulator for design/training, found (limited search; absence is not proof).
- Game-physics-in-engineering is NOT novel: Holz 2014 (PBD granular for excavator training), Servin 2021a (real-time earthmoving vs DEM, 10-25 %), Schaefer 2024 (PBF crack sealing, validated to a lab experiment), Gupta 2025. The user explicitly said: if it is not novel, do not claim it. Do NOT list it as an innovation. At most one neutral sentence in the lineage section stating the PBD family originates in computer graphics [Muller2007, MacklinMuller2013, Macklin2014, Macklin2016, Bender2014] and has precedents in engineering [Holz2014, Schaefer2024].
- Not found anywhere: PBD/XPBD/PBF for ball mills or mineral comminution; PBF validation against analytic viscous flow; direct validation of PBD granular flow against DEM. MillDynamics3's own analytic gates (docs/VERIFICATION.md) fill part of that gap.
